Demand for capacity and precision is rising

Mobile traffic, compute infrastructure and resilient positioning are all growing, and they put pressure on the same transport and timing layers.

Traffic: rising traffic strengthens the case for scalable optical transport and high-capacity interconnection. PNT: build a complementary LEO PNT layer using optical ranging, timing and the same infrastructure that carries communications.

The gap is deployment, optical networking and resilience

High-speed connectivity is available, but extending it efficiently and building resilient PNT infrastructure remain difficult. The gap is no longer only about speed.

The gap: scalable optical connectivity and resilient, high-precision PNT in one infrastructure layer.

StarConnectNet is a proposed architecture. Commercial deployment and performance validation remain future work.

Existing solutions each solve part of the problem

Fiber, RF, FSO, GNSS and LEO-PNT leave gaps in deployment flexibility, scalable optical networking and precision PNT.

Opportunity: optical LOS network + LEO pathway + SC-PNT. No single mature infrastructure combines scalable optical connectivity with integrated precision PNT. SCN is not replacing fiber, RF or GNSS. It adds a new optical networking layer and a complementary PNT capability.

Existing FSO solutions and their limits

High-capacity optical links are proven, but deployment, availability and network scalability remain challenges.

The gap: FSO provides exceptional link capacity, but scalable, dynamically routed optical LOS infrastructure remains an open deployment challenge.

Where we are and what we prove next

The key step is converting analytical validation into measured optical-system evidence, from TRL 2 toward TRL 3.

Status: no integrated optical POC yet. Experimental BER, throughput and timing validation remain to be demonstrated.

Plan of action

Completed: ideation and concept building. Current: developing the proof of concept. Next: validate.

Roadmap logic: prove the link at short range first, validate scaling, integrate satellite-assisted networking, then launch an MVP. Distance targets and satellite validation remain subject to experimental results and engineering feasibility.
